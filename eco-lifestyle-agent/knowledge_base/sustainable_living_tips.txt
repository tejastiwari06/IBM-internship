SUSTAINABLE LIVING TIPS - ECO LIFESTYLE KNOWLEDGE BASE
=========================================================

CATEGORY: Reducing Plastic Use at Home
---------------------------------------
1. Replace single-use plastic bags with reusable cloth or jute bags when shopping.
2. Use a reusable water bottle (stainless steel or glass) instead of buying bottled water.
3. Switch to bamboo or compostable toothbrushes instead of plastic ones.
4. Buy in bulk to reduce individual plastic packaging waste.
5. Use beeswax wraps or silicone lids instead of cling wrap (plastic wrap).
6. Choose bar soap and shampoo bars over liquid products in plastic bottles.
7. Replace plastic straws with metal, bamboo, or glass straws.
8. Use cloth napkins and reusable rags instead of paper towels.
9. Store food in glass jars and stainless steel containers instead of plastic containers.
10. Opt for loose-leaf tea with a metal strainer instead of plastic-wrapped tea bags.

CATEGORY: Energy Conservation at Home
---------------------------------------
1. Switch to LED bulbs — they use 75% less energy than incandescent bulbs and last 25 times longer.
2. Install a programmable or smart thermostat to reduce heating and cooling energy use.
3. Unplug electronics and chargers when not in use — "phantom loads" can account for 10% of home electricity use.
4. Use cold water for laundry — 90% of washing machine energy goes to heating water.
5. Air-dry clothes instead of using a dryer; it saves around 4 lbs of CO2 per load.
6. Seal windows and doors to prevent drafts and reduce heating/cooling energy needs.
7. Install solar panels if feasible — they can reduce electricity bills by up to 70%.
8. Use natural daylight as much as possible and turn off lights when leaving a room.
9. Choose energy-efficient appliances (look for Energy Star certified products).
10. Take shorter showers — reducing by just 2 minutes can save up to 10 gallons of water.

CATEGORY: Reducing Food Waste
-------------------------------
1. Plan your meals weekly before grocery shopping to buy only what you need.
2. Store food properly — keep fruits and vegetables in proper conditions to extend shelf life.
3. Practice FIFO (First In, First Out) — use older food items before newer ones.
4. Compost food scraps like vegetable peels, coffee grounds, and eggshells instead of throwing them away.
5. Learn to cook with leftovers creatively — yesterday's roasted vegetables can become today's stir-fry.
6. Buy "ugly" or imperfect produce — it tastes the same and reduces food waste at farms.
7. Freeze bread, fruits, and other perishables before they go bad.
8. Understand the difference between "best before" and "use by" dates — many foods are safe past "best before."
9. Grow your own herbs and vegetables in a kitchen garden or balcony garden.
10. Donate surplus non-perishable food to local food banks or community pantries.

CATEGORY: Water Conservation
------------------------------
1. Fix leaky faucets immediately — a single dripping tap can waste 3,000+ gallons per year.
2. Install low-flow showerheads and faucet aerators to reduce water usage.
3. Collect rainwater in a barrel for watering plants and gardens.
4. Water plants in the early morning or evening to minimize evaporation.
5. Use drought-resistant or native plants in your garden that require less watering.
6. Run dishwashers and washing machines only when fully loaded.
7. Turn off the tap while brushing teeth or washing hands — saves up to 8 gallons per day.
8. Use a broom instead of a garden hose to clean driveways and sidewalks.
9. Install dual-flush toilets or place a water-saving device in your toilet tank.
10. Take shorter showers and consider installing a shower timer.

CATEGORY: Sustainable Transportation
--------------------------------------
1. Walk or cycle for short trips — cycling 10 km instead of driving saves approximately 1.5 kg of CO2.
2. Use public transportation — buses and trains produce far less CO2 per passenger than private cars.
3. Carpool with colleagues or neighbors to reduce the number of vehicles on the road.
4. Consider an electric vehicle (EV) or hybrid car for your next vehicle purchase.
5. Work from home when possible — each day of remote work saves an average commute emission.
6. Combine errands into one trip to reduce overall driving distance.
7. Maintain your vehicle properly — well-maintained tires and engine improve fuel efficiency.
8. Offset unavoidable flight emissions by contributing to verified carbon offset programs.
9. Choose trains over short-haul flights — trains emit 6x less CO2 per passenger kilometer.
10. Use a cargo bike or electric scooter for urban deliveries and shopping.

CATEGORY: Sustainable Shopping & Consumption
---------------------------------------------
1. Buy secondhand — thrift stores, online platforms, and swap events give products a second life.
2. Follow the "one-in, one-out" rule — donate or sell an item before buying a new one.
3. Repair rather than replace — learn basic sewing, electronics repair, and furniture restoration.
4. Choose quality over quantity — well-made items last longer and create less waste.
5. Avoid fast fashion — choose sustainable clothing brands or buy vintage.
6. Check product certifications: Fair Trade, Organic, B Corp, FSC for wood products.
7. Borrow or rent items you rarely use (tools, party supplies, camping gear).
8. Support local farmers markets and businesses to reduce transportation emissions.
9. Choose products with minimal or recyclable packaging.
10. Participate in community sharing programs, tool libraries, and buy-nothing groups.

CATEGORY: Home Composting
---------------------------
1. Start a compost bin or pile in your backyard — browns (leaves, cardboard) + greens (food scraps) + moisture.
2. Use a worm bin (vermicomposting) for apartment composting — it is odorless and efficient.
3. Avoid composting meat, dairy, oily foods, and diseased plants.
4. Turn your compost pile every 1–2 weeks to speed up decomposition.
5. Finished compost enriches garden soil and reduces the need for chemical fertilizers.
6. Bokashi composting ferments ALL food waste including meat and dairy — great for small spaces.
7. Municipal food scrap collection programs are available in many cities — check local guidelines.
8. Compost tea (liquid from compost bins) can be diluted and used as fertilizer for plants.
