RECYCLING GUIDELINES & WASTE MANAGEMENT - ECO LIFESTYLE KNOWLEDGE BASE
========================================================================

GENERAL RECYCLING RULES (Universal)
--------------------------------------
- Always rinse food and liquid residue from containers before recycling.
- Remove caps and lids unless local guidelines say otherwise (many programs now accept them on).
- Do not bag recyclables in plastic bags unless your program specifically requests it.
- When in doubt, throw it out — contamination ruins entire recycling batches.
- Flatten cardboard boxes to save space in recycling bins.
- Check your local municipal website for specific accepted materials — rules vary by city.

WHAT GOES IN THE RECYCLING BIN (Generally Accepted)
------------------------------------------------------
PAPER:
- Newspapers, magazines, books (remove covers)
- Office paper, envelopes (remove plastic windows if possible)
- Cardboard and corrugated boxes (flatten them)
- Cereal boxes and paperboard packaging
- Paper bags
NOTE: Greasy pizza boxes are NOT recyclable — compost them or trash them.

PLASTICS:
- Plastic bottles (water, soda, juice, shampoo) — usually #1 PET and #2 HDPE
- Milk jugs and detergent bottles
- Plastic tubs (yogurt, butter, margarine containers)
- Most rigid plastic containers
NOTE: Plastic bags, wrap, and film (#4 LDPE) are NOT curbside recyclable — return them to grocery store drop-offs.

METALS:
- Aluminum cans (beverage and food cans)
- Steel/tin cans (soup, vegetable, pet food cans)
- Aluminum foil and trays (clean, not greasy)
- Small metal items (bottle caps, lids)

GLASS:
- Glass bottles and jars (food and beverage) — clear, green, and brown
- Rinse clean before recycling
NOTE: Broken glass, window glass, mirrors, and glass cookware (Pyrex) are NOT recyclable curbside.

WHAT DOES NOT GO IN THE RECYCLING BIN
---------------------------------------
- Plastic bags and film (take to store drop-off)
- Styrofoam (polystyrene) — check for special drop-off programs
- Bubble wrap (store drop-off for plastic film)
- Pizza boxes with grease (compost the top, trash the greasy bottom)
- Disposable coffee cups (most have plastic lining — not recyclable)
- Ceramics, pottery, and dishware
- Electronic waste (e-waste)
- Batteries — hazardous waste
- Lightbulbs (CFL, fluorescent — hazardous waste)
- Medical waste and sharps
- Diapers and personal hygiene products
- Clothes and textiles (donate or textile recycling bin)
- Food waste (compost bin)

SPECIAL DISPOSAL PROGRAMS
---------------------------
ELECTRONICS (E-WASTE):
- Computers, laptops, tablets, smartphones, TVs, monitors
- Batteries (rechargeable and single-use)
- Programs: Manufacturer take-back programs, Best Buy drop-off, Staples recycling, local e-waste collection events.
- In India: CPCB-registered e-waste recyclers; many brand stores accept old devices.
- Why it matters: E-waste contains lead, mercury, and cadmium — hazardous if landfilled.

BATTERIES:
- Car batteries: Auto parts stores (AutoZone, O'Reilly) accept them free.
- Household batteries: Many hardware stores, Home Depot, IKEA accept them.
- Rechargeable batteries: Call2Recycle program has 34,000+ drop-off locations in North America.

HAZARDOUS HOUSEHOLD WASTE (HHW):
- Paint (latex and oil), solvents, pesticides, herbicides, fertilizers
- Motor oil and antifreeze
- Pool chemicals, fluorescent bulbs, thermometers with mercury
- Disposal: Local HHW collection events or permanent HHW facilities.
- Motor oil: Many auto parts stores accept used motor oil for recycling.

TEXTILES & CLOTHING:
- Donate wearable items to Goodwill, Salvation Army, local shelters, or Poshmark/ThredUp.
- Worn-out textiles: H&M, Patagonia, North Face, and many brands have take-back programs.
- Terracycle offers mail-in solutions for hard-to-recycle items.
- Never put textiles in curbside recycling — they tangle machinery.

COMPOSTING:
- Accepted: Fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, tea bags, eggshells, grass clippings, leaves, cardboard.
- NOT accepted in home compost: meat, fish, dairy, oily foods (use industrial composting or Bokashi for these).
- Many cities offer municipal composting programs — look for the green bin or compost pickup service.

MEDICAL WASTE & SHARPS:
- Never dispose of needles/syringes in regular trash — use a sharps container.
- Many pharmacies offer sharps disposal.
- Expired medications: Take to pharmacy drug take-back programs (never flush down the toilet).

RECYCLING SYMBOLS EXPLAINED
-----------------------------
#1 PET (Polyethylene Terephthalate) — Water/soda bottles, clamshells. WIDELY RECYCLED.
#2 HDPE (High-Density Polyethylene) — Milk jugs, detergent bottles. WIDELY RECYCLED.
#3 PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride) — Pipes, blister packs. RARELY RECYCLED.
#4 LDPE (Low-Density Polyethylene) — Plastic bags, film. STORE DROP-OFF ONLY.
#5 PP (Polypropylene) — Yogurt tubs, bottle caps. CHECK LOCALLY (increasingly accepted).
#6 PS (Polystyrene) — Styrofoam, disposable cups. RARELY RECYCLED.
#7 Other — Mixed/layered plastics. RARELY RECYCLED.

TIPS FOR BETTER RECYCLING
---------------------------
1. When possible, buy products that are already made from recycled materials — closes the recycling loop.
2. Crush aluminum cans but do not crush plastic bottles (facilities need to identify them).
3. Remove staples from paper if possible.
4. Labels on glass and plastic do not need to be removed.
5. Use the Earth911 website (earth911.com) or RecycleNation to find local recycling options.
6. In India, use the iRecycle app or CPCB portal to find nearest recycling points.
